# The support-call checklist

Keeping this page open is the whole trick. Every call goes through the same four phases: **Take the call → Triage → Stabilize → Close.** The red/yellow/green scale comes from nursing triage, and it works on tech too: it stops you from sprinting on a paper jam while a scammer is emptying a savings account.

## Phase 1 — Take the call

- [ ] Answer like it's welcome, not like it's noise. First sentence: *"Hey! What are we fixing?"*
- [ ] Say the time expectation out loud: *"Five minutes if it's small, if not we'll book Sunday."* A named end time prevents both sighs.
- [ ] Write the problem in your own words (a note app is fine, paper is better) — you will need it at the close.

## Phase 2 — Triage: the five questions

Ask, in this order, and let silences sit:

1. **"What are you trying to do?"** — Not "what's wrong." People report symptoms; you need the goal.
2. **"What happened just before?"** — "It worked this morning" has a middle.
3. **"When did it last work right?"**
4. **"Did anything change?"** — New phone, new password, cousin visiting, software update.
5. **"What does the error say, word for word?"** — Have them read it aloud. Do not guess from memory. An exact phrase ("We've detected unusual activity") is half the diagnosis.

Then sort the call red / yellow / green:

- **RED — money or identity, right now.** Someone is on the phone asking for money/code/gift cards; they already paid a "support" call; a text about a package they didn't order; the bank called *them*. → Drop everything else. Go to the [scam shields](06-elder-scam-shields.md). Nothing below matters until RED is handled.
- **YELLOW — blocked from something needed today.** Can't refill a prescription online, can't join a video appointment, phone won't charge, no internet before a deadline. → Stabilize now with the [rescue order](04-rescue-order.md).
- **GREEN — annoying, not blocking.** Printer makes banded copies, tablet is slow, they want to change a ringtone. → This is teaching territory ([chapter 2](02-teach-without-taking-over.md)); book Sunday if the call has run long.

One important RED check before any fixing: **"Is anyone you don't trust on your screen right now, or did you give anyone access by phone or app?"** If yes — hang up on *them*, not on you. Nothing to uninstall yet; just stop all contact first.

## Phase 3 — Stabilize (before you fix)

- [ ] **It's plugged in and it's on.** Say it kindly, because half of everything ever.
- [ ] **Full close and reopen of the app** (not just the home screen — swipe the app away entirely, or close the window).
- [ ] **Reboot the device.** Not "restart later." All the way off and back on.
- [ ] **If the internet is involved:** [the rescue order](04-rescue-order.md) — wifi, then phone/e-mail, then printer.
- [ ] **Take a screenshot together** of whatever the screen says now. It becomes the "before" picture and the teaching prop.

If three stabilizer moves haven't changed anything, stop pulling further levers over the phone. Say so out loud: *"We've done the honest resets. This wants eyes on it or a real appointment."*

## Phase 4 — Close the call (never skip)

- [ ] One-sentence summary in *their* words: *"So the photos weren't gone, they were filing up the phone instead of printing."* You don't need a perfect diagnosis to close well — you need one shared sentence both of you agree is true.
- [ ] **Their next step, theirs, out loud:** *"Tomorrow, open the gmail app first thing and read me the first line if it does it again."*
- [ ] **Your next step, out loud:** *"I'll text you tonight to check it's still good."* (Then do.)
- [ ] Book Sunday if anything is still open. Put it in the calendar *now*, on the call.
- [ ] Log it: date, person, problem in your words, fix, still-open threads. A note app titled "Family Help Log" is plenty. Six months in, this log is the thing that gets a repeat problem solved in ninety seconds.
- [ ] Last words are warm and earn the next call: *"No worry at all. This is literally what I'm for."*

## The two phrases worth memorizing

- **"Let's look at it together on Sunday"** — the phrase that turns a marathon into an appointment.
- **"Before we spend money on this, one more thing to try"** — the phrase that keeps a Green from becoming a $99 repair the pro upsold on Tuesday.
