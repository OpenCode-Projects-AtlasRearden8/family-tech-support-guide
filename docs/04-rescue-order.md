# The rescue order: wifi, then phone, then printer

When the call opens with *"the printer, my phone, and the internet are all broken,"* do not take the three problems. Take one — the order solves the other two more often than not.

**The rescue order is: 1. wifi, 2. the phone, 3. the printer.**

Same as the hospital room: you check breathing before the paper cuts. Your family member will name the printer first, because the printer is the thing that *hurt last*. Don't mind the order they say things in. Mind the order the house depends on things in.

- **Wifi first** because a dead connection *looks like* everything else being broken: no email, no photos loading, five phone apps "acting weird," and yes — the printer showing offline.
- **The phone second**, because it's their window to medicine refills, the family, and the two-factor codes we set up in [chapter 5](05-passwords-and-backups.md).
- **The printer last**, because nothing in a house actually depends on a printer working *today*. It's the most fiddlesome device in any home and the least worth a fresh hour of patience. Also — politely — it's usually not broken at all.

## Rescue 1: the internet is out

Run this in order, out loud, so it becomes teachable later (the [Wi-Fi teach sheet](teach-sheets/wifi.md) is this ritual, printable, for the cabinet by the router):

1. **Scope it.** *"Does the TV work? Does the tablet?"* All devices off = house/router problem. One device off = that device's problem (most often just Wi-Fi toggled off, airplane mode, or a forgotten network).
2. **Look at the little lights.** Modem and router: which lights are on, which are red or blinking weird? Take a photo — it's the "before" picture and the exact thing the ISP call will ask about.
3. **The ritual: unplug the router and the modem.** Both. From *power*, not from anything else. Sixty full seconds — count them, this is not optional. (That minute feels silly. It isn't: the modem genuinely needs the quiet to finish its reset, and a timer on the phone makes the wait teachable.)
4. **Plug the modem in first.** Wait until its lights settle (2–3 minutes). Then the router. Then wait again until the Wi-Fi name reappears.
5. **Test by the lights, then by a device.** Wi-Fi name shows up on the phone, select it, done.
6. **Still down?** Check whether the neighbors / another floor have service, then follow the [ISP chapter](07-when-to-call-the-isp-or-a-pro.md) — that's its jurisdiction now, with your reset ritual already done (which is exactly what their call script asks you to complete first, so that's half the call).

**One rescue move, not five:** unplug-wait-replug is the ritual. Don't stack password changes, new app installs, or "reset to factory" on top of a dead line. Factory resets delete things. Never as a first move, rarely as a last.

## Rescue 2: the phone is the problem

In order, the four honest phone rescues:

1. **Reboot all the way.** Half of phone weirdness is a sleeping, overworked week of apps. Show the full power-down (not just the screen lock).
2. **Charging.** Half of "the battery is dying" is the charger: lint packed into the port (a flashlight and a wooden toothpick, gently), the old cable that only charges at one magic angle, the wrong brick in the wall. Swap in a known-good cable and brick before you blame the battery — it is the cheap test first, and battery replacements are the expensive one.
3. **Storage.** *"Storage almost full"* makes a phone slow, and slow looks broken. Delete the obvious photos, empty "recently deleted," then Settings shows which apps eat the rest (photos, mostly, always).
4. **Update, then stop insisting on it.** An update two months behind causes the "nothing works right" call; the update the phone begs for is the fix. But never during the rescue — updates take twenty minutes and a good charger, so schedule *that* on the [phone teach sheet](teach-sheets/phone-basics.md).

## Rescue 3: the printer (the polite long play)

The printer is nobody's priority. When you get to it, enjoy these facts: most "broken printers" are one of a short list of boring things, and the [printer teach sheet](teach-sheets/printer.md) covers them all in one page. Quick version, in order of likelihood:

1. It's asleep or in a sulk. Power it fully off, all-the-way, for a minute. This fixes a shocking amount.
2. Wrong printer selected. Phones and laptops quietly remember old copies of a printer, or the office one from a decade ago. Check the printer *name* on the print screen and choose the one that is actually here.
3. Stuck queue. One jammed or half-dead print job holds every job behind it hostage. Open the printer queue on the device, delete everything in it, print again.
4. Paper or ink in the wrong place: photo paper loaded in the main tray, a dried-out colour cartridge, or "draft" mode printing so faintly nobody believes it printed.
5. USB cable or Wi-Fi name changed (they now have "NETGEAR-ext" freshly printed all over the counters after a router move — offer to reconnect it on the *next* visit, printer-peace is more important than printer-now).

Then the standard-close from [chapter 1](01-support-call-checklist.md): *"Printers test themselves — tell me tomorrow if the test page came out. Sunday we teach the phone to print."*

## Saying the order out loud

The order does double duty when you say it to them, because it reassures the part of them that thinks everything got worse:

> "Internet first — it makes the phone and the printer look sick. Then phone. Then printer, even though the printer was your villain tonight. Usually an hour or two from now, two of your three fix themselves the moment the internet's back."
