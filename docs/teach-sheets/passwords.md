# Teach sheet: passwords, the kind way

*The friendly rule book for passwords. Keep it with the phone — never written on the phone.*

**The big idea:** one app remembers passwords *so you don't have to*. It's already in the phone — the phone's password keeper (on iPhone it's iCloud/Passwords; on Android it's the Google Password Manager). You only need to remember one thing in the whole world now.

**The one password to remember — make it words, not symbols:**

- Pick **four small words that have nothing to do with each other**: *maple piano basket fly*
- Write them **on paper once**, in your notebook, in the drawer. Not in the phone, not in a text, not in "Passwords.docx" on the desktop. Paper in the drawer is respectful storage for the one password.
- That's the whole trick: *four words* beat anything with !@# in it, and you can say them to yourself while you type.

**Two house rules (they protect everything):**
1. **A code that arrives in a text is a fire alarm.** Never read it to anyone who calls or texts out of the blue — no bank, not "Microsoft," not a grandchild in trouble. Codes are for you typing them *in the moment*, into the screen in front of you.
2. **Nobody helps by "just logging in for you."** Not on the phone, not by remote control, not even family. You type. Everyone else waits politely.

**When a password fails:**

- Tap **"Forgot password?"** on the *first* try, not the fourth. Guessing is how accounts lock themselves.
- Password reset always goes to your email — which is why your email password is the treasure of the house. If it's been a while, we spend ten minutes on it on the next visit.
- If a site "forgot" to save your password — tap *Save* when the phone offers. The phone is collecting for you, going forward, one login at a time.

**Where the four words live:** in your head, with one paper copy in the drawer — and nowhere else. Not on this sheet, not in the phone, not ("just for now") in a note.
