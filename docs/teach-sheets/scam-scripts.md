# Teach sheet: what to say when a scam calls

*Tape it inside a kitchen cabinet, at eye level, so you never need to search for the words. A scam only works in the ten seconds before you find your words — this page *is* your words.*

**Three sentences to say (whatever the story is):**

1. **"I don't do banking or computer things over the phone. Goodbye."** (Then hang up. Down like you mean it — no discussion needs to happen first.)
2. **"Let me call my daughter/son/friend before anything moves."** (Hang up and dial your real person from *your contacts*, not from any number they gave you.)
3. **"I'm calling the number on my card back."** (Your bank's real number is on your card and on the month's statement. Not from a text, not from a call, not "be right back" from whoever's on the line.)

**Then the three big tells — if any one shows up, it's a scam:**

- **Gift cards.** Any story with "gift cards" in it — IRS, hospital, utility, your bank, a prize: 100% a scam. There is no other version, ever.
- **Urgency.** "Right now," "ten minutes," "the account freezes today." Real companies wait for you to call back on your own number. Only scammers need *this minute*.
- **Codes and access.** Nobody legitimate needs a text code read to them, or "a minute of access to your computer." Speaking any code out loud hands over the keys to the whole house.

**Family calls and AI voices:** voices can be faked now — faces, video, all of it. The one check that still works: **hang up and call that person yourself, on your own phone.** If it's the family emergency, use the family code word — said out loud, never texted.

**Family code word (spoken between us, never written):** ________________________________ *(in memory only — for real this time)*

**If it turns out money already moved:** call your bank *now* (number off your card), keep every message and receipt, and go through them together with your person — not with whoever is still on the phone. Scammers did the scamming; they hold the whole blame. This page is just the "what we do next."

**Report it (five minutes, helps the next person):** reportfraud.ftc.gov — and tell me so I can lock the email down same-day.

*Tip for printing: one page at 100%.*
